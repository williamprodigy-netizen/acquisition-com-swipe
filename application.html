The application

An application to be invested in, that sells you a workshop when you fail

JotForm 231197990495167, embedded in a HubSpot page at /apply. The headline is “WE SCALE GREAT COMPANIES.” Nothing about it reads as an order form.

The mechanic, verbatim

Based on your answers, you’d need to grow in order to qualify as a portfolio company… and we’d like to help. Would you like us to show you exactly what we’d do to scale your company? If so, click the button below to attend a paid workshop at our Headquarters in Las Vegas. JotForm 231197990495167, question 14 · VERIFIED

You did not come to buy a workshop. You came to be considered for investment — the highest status thing they offer. You answered revenue and profit three separate ways. Then the form told you, based on your own numbers, that you are not big enough yet.

The $5,000 seat arrives as the remedy for a gap you just proved to yourself. That is a completely different psychological position from “buy my workshop”, and it is why they can charge $5,000 with no guarantee and no discount. READ

The ordering is the trick

Qualification comes before contact

Revenue is Q10-11. EBITDA is Q12-13 and again at Q15. Email is Q19. Phone is Q20. They make you invest nineteen answers before they will take an address — and by then the sunk cost makes finishing near-automatic.

Profit asked three ways

Annual revenue, 12-month EBITDA, then 3-month EBITDA. The short window catches anyone inflating the annual number, and the EBITDA framing itself filters — an owner who cannot answer it is not the buyer.

The example answer teaches the format

“Ex: I help dentists get more patients using facebook ads for $2000 per month.” One line that gets them a clean, gradeable answer instead of a paragraph of waffle.

Even the NO captures

Q22’s decline option says out loud that you will “only be added to our email list”. There is no exit from this form that is not a list add.
